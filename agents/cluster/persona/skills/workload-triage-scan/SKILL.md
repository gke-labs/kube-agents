# Task

Answer a cluster-wide "what is failing / is it healthy" question from one batched read, finish at once when nothing is unhealthy, and otherwise name each finding's cause through the focused skill that owns its symptom.

# Workflow

1. Run the scan once, from this skill's directory (the base directory the harness gives when it loads the skill), where `scripts/workload_triage.py` is. It reads pods, Deployments, StatefulSets, DaemonSets, Jobs and Warning events across all namespaces in three batched `kubectl get -o json` reads and prints only what needs attention:

   ```bash
   python3 scripts/workload_triage.py
   ```

   Pass `--include-system` on that scan only when the question names a system component or system namespace. It ends with `unhealthy: <count>` (unhealthy pods plus controllers below desired, outside system namespaces) followed by `system unhealthy: <count>` (unhealthy pods plus controllers below desired in folded `kube-*`, `gke-*`, `gmp-*` and `*-system` namespaces). Its rules, for reading the objects yourself where the script cannot run: a pod with `deletionTimestamp` set (`Terminating`) or `Pending` behind `SchedulingGated` is initializing regardless of age; a pod whose age (`now - creationTimestamp`) is under 10 minutes is initializing when it is `Pending`, waiting in `ContainerCreating`/`PodInitializing`, or `NotReady`; a pod is unhealthy when a container waits in `CrashLoopBackOff`, `RunContainerError`, an image pull error or a config error, when its age is at least 10 minutes and it is `Pending`, waiting in `ContainerCreating`/`PodInitializing` or `NotReady`, when it is Ready now after a container restarted in the last 60 minutes (`RecentRestart`), or when its phase is `Failed` unless either its owning `Job` has completed or it ended more than 60 minutes ago and is owned by a `ReplicaSet`, `StatefulSet` or `DaemonSet`; a `Deployment`, `StatefulSet` or `DaemonSet` with ready below desired, or an inactive, incomplete `Job` with condition `Failed=True`, counts under controllers below desired; Warning events count from the last 60 minutes. Exit code 2 means a cluster read failed or returned truncated or unparseable output: stop and report as [`references/reporting.md`](references/reporting.md) ("Cannot proceed") describes, quoting the exact stderr line.

2. Check `WARNING EVENTS` for any row with no matching `UNHEALTHY PODS` row whose `STATE` is `gone` or `-` (the event's object is no longer present or is not a scanned pod or controller — skip rows whose `STATE` is `healthy`, `initializing` or `stale_failed`, which are already up, still within startup grace, or superseded): route `FailedScheduling` to `pod-pending-scheduling`, and route `FailedMount`, `ProvisioningFailed` and `FailedAttachVolume` to `pod-container-creating`. Because neither `gone` nor `-` has a live pod to read, skip the target skill's step 1 `kubectl get pod`: for a `gone` pod row, start at step 2 (`events` filtered by `involvedObject.name`) and read the owning workload's current pods if needed to confirm whether a replacement is running; for a `-` non-pod row (such as `PersistentVolumeClaim/<name>`), start at step 2 (`events` for that object's name) and the target skill's object-kind step. Quote all other `WARNING EVENTS` rows (including `healthy`, `initializing` and `stale_failed` ones) as the event states them without loading a focused skill.

3. If the scan ends with `unhealthy: 0` and `system unhealthy: 0` and step 2 routed no warning event to a focused skill, stop here and run no further command. Report the summary line (pods healthy, initializing, controllers below desired, warning events), any initializing pods by name, any `WARNING EVENTS` rows, the `RESTARTED EARLIER` and `FAILED EARLIER` rows when the question is about restarts or failures, and the system-namespace lines as written (they carry restart counts for system pods). This is the status question with nothing failing that SOUL.md §2 answers in one sentence plus the evidence that settles it, and the scan's first line (kubeconfig context and UTC time), its summary line and the command are that evidence. Re-listing pods, events or controllers to confirm a clean scan adds nothing. If `unhealthy: 0` and `system unhealthy: N` (`N > 0`) and step 2 routed no warning event, stop here and run no further command, but do not report `"No failing workloads."`: report that non-system namespaces have 0 unhealthy workloads while folded namespaces have `N` unhealthy pods or controllers below desired, quoting the summary line and the `SYSTEM NAMESPACES AND NODES` lines as written. When the question did not ask about system namespaces, there is no next step: omit any `[next: ...]` line (never offer or run `--include-system`) and stop your turn after reporting.

4. Otherwise, group the `UNHEALTHY PODS` rows by owning workload (the pod name minus its ReplicaSet or ordinal suffix). Pods of one workload share a cause: investigate one representative pod.

5. Take up to three workloads, ordered by the `STATUS` column `UNHEALTHY PODS` prints: `CrashLoopBackOff`, `RunContainerError`, `Evicted`/`Failed` or `RecentRestart` first (using `LAST EXIT` such as `137 OOMKilled` or `1 Error` to break ties), then `Unschedulable`/`Pending`, then `ImagePullBackOff`/`ErrImagePull`/`InvalidImageName`/`ErrImageNeverPull`, `CreateContainerConfigError`/`CreateContainerError` and `ContainerCreating`/`PodInitializing`, then `NotReady`. Load the skill in the row's `SKILL` column and follow it for the representative pod until the cause is named, usually its first two steps. List any further workloads with their scan row only, and say they were not investigated.

6. `RESTARTED EARLIER` rows (Ready now, last restart before the events window) and `FAILED EARLIER` rows (pods from a completed `Job`, or replaced pods that ended before the window) are history. List them when the question asks about restarts or failures; do not investigate them.

7. For a `Job/<name>` row marked `failed: <reason>` in `CONTROLLERS BELOW DESIRED` with no matching `UNHEALTHY PODS` row (a failed `Job` whose pods the controller removed, such as `DeadlineExceeded` or `BackoffLimitExceeded` under `restartPolicy: OnFailure`), inspect `kubectl get job <name> -n <namespace> -o json` and its namespace events (`kubectl get events -n <namespace> --field-selector involvedObject.name=<name>`) to report the cause and proposed fix. For any other controller in `CONTROLLERS BELOW DESIRED` with no matching `UNHEALTHY PODS` row, first check `WARNING EVENTS` for `FailedCreate` in that namespace and, whenever pods are missing (`INITIALIZING` rows do not account for the gap) or `FailedCreate` is present, read the controller's `.status.conditions` (`kubectl get <kind>/<name> -n <namespace> -o jsonpath='{.status.conditions}'` for `ReplicaFailure`) and `kubectl get events -n <namespace> --field-selector reason=FailedCreate`: if `ReplicaFailure` or `FailedCreate` is present, report the quota, admission webhook or policy denial cause and proposed fix; report the controller as rolling out or initializing (with the counts as read, without waiting) only when no creation failure exists.

8. Record the result as [`references/reporting.md`](references/reporting.md) describes.

# Rules

- Keep the scan itself to about six commands, the script included. Each workload step 5 selects adds its focused skill's steps on top of that, usually two commands.
- Report the current state only. Never `sleep`, `--watch`, or re-run the scan to see whether a pod comes up. A pod in `INITIALIZING` is reported as initializing.
- Do not explore system namespaces (`kube-*`, `gke-*`, `gmp-*`, `*-system`) or node events. Quote their summary lines as written, including any `(+N more ...)` tail. Pass `--include-system` on step 1's scan only when the question names a system component or system namespace; never run a second scan with `--include-system` after `unhealthy: 0`, and never append `[next: ...]` offering to check system workloads when the question did not ask about system namespaces.
- Do not list pods, nodes or controllers again in other shapes. The scan output is the inventory.
