# The per-entry readiness rules

The rules `upgrade_readiness.EXTRA_RULES` runs after the PDB, webhook, maintenance and skew
rules of `fleet_upgrade_report.py --readiness`, one module each under `scripts/readiness_rules/`
(the package docstring is the contract). A blocker joins the member's verdict and prints under
`other blockers`; a risk never moves the verdict and prints under `risks`; each cell is
`<rule id>: <the rule's text>`, and a rule's note (what it read on a clean member) prints in the
note column behind the same prefix. The catalogue is the upgrade-failure catalogue in the
repository's design docs; an incident scenario is one of the support-case rules that reads a
project-level inventory.

| Rule id                         | Catalogue entry or incident scenario                                                       | Grade   | What it reads                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    | The action in the finding                                                                                                                                                                      |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `control-plane-upgrade-history` | Entry 2, the control plane's side: an upgrade or repair that failed on compute capacity    | risk    | `gcloud container operations list` once per project: `DONE` `UPGRADE_MASTER`, `UPGRADE_NODES` or `REPAIR_CLUSTER` operations on the member or on one of its node pools in the last 90 days whose error names capacity; the cluster's `maintenancePolicy.window`                                                                                                                  | Set a maintenance window in an off-peak hour for the region and retry the upgrade in it; keep upgrade notifications consumed.                                                                  |
| `cluster-status-age`            | Entry 5, read before the window: a cluster held by an operation that has not ended         | blocker | The cluster's `status` and, from the same operations list, the operations on the member or on one of its node pools (a pool upgrade, resize or repair names the pool); a status other than `RUNNING` with an operation `RUNNING` for more than four hours blocks, naming it, a younger one is noted; no `RUNNING` operation at all blocks, quoting the cluster's `statusMessage` | Freeze changes; past a few hours open a support case quoting the operation id, or the status and its message when nothing is `RUNNING` (the `gke-upgrades` skill's "Stuck operation" section). |

A blocking rule declares what its mechanism holds (`HOLDS`): a control-plane move, a node-pool
move, or both. The fold stamps it on each `rule_blocking` row as `holds`, and the Monday audit's
`upgrade-blocked` check counts the block for the move the cluster's lag makes due:
`lb-connection-draining` and `orphan-node-objects` hold a node's removal and do not count where
the control plane alone is due, as a budget does not; `cluster-status-age` and
`target-patch-valid` hold both moves.

A failed project or location read is an error row under the table (exit 1), and the rules that
need it say `unknown`; a rule that can block then leaves the member `unknown`, one that cannot
is reported in the note. The three list reads are projected to the fields the rules use, so a
project of a few thousand instances stays under the credential proxy's output cap; a list the
proxy cut is the same error, naming the cap.
